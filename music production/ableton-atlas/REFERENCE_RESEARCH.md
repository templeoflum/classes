# Reference recordings and Live’s origins

September 21, 2026 · Private authoring record

## Scope

The current prototype's Approaches and Lineages start with Headlock, Lost Weekend, and Ableton Live, but the user has rejected their treatment as insufficiently connected and intuitive. This is the first credits-and-context pass, not a completed listening analysis or an accepted editorial structure. The previous Oram/Reich research remains valid within its recorded limits but is no longer the prototype’s historical starting point.

Follow-up: [Session / Arrangement — a connected section](SESSION_ARRANGEMENT_RESEARCH.md) develops one evidence-backed relationship between Live's operation, musical use, keyboard navigation, and origins. It includes an Operator design-history lead. This is a private blueprint, not an implemented replacement; the interface is unchanged by this research pass.

## Headlock

- [Artist’s track page](https://imogenheap.bandcamp.com/track/headlock): official listening reference. Its 2012 Bandcamp date is not the original album date. [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1785686644) identifies the 2005 album release.
- [Published track credits](https://readdork.com/track/imogen-heap-headlock-2006): Heap’s production/programming work, Mich Gerber’s double bass, and Richie Mills’s backing vocals support the initial people/material map. This is a secondary credit source; corroboration against original liner notes remains desirable. The artist’s older credits page could not be retrieved.
- [Heap’s Sound on Sound interview](https://www.soundonsound.com/people/imogen-heap-recording-ellipse): read for her account of writing and producing Speak for Yourself together, and the earlier Frou Frou collaboration with Guy Sigsworth. Its detailed production walkthrough is for Ellipse, not Headlock. Do not transfer those settings, effects, or instruments to this track.

Next depth: locate track-specific session evidence and annotate audible layers/entrances. No exact synthesizer patch, sampling method, or effect chain is asserted. Do not import Hide and Seek’s vocal-processing story.

## Lost Weekend

- [Artist’s title-track page](https://phoebebridgers.bandcamp.com/track/lost-weekend) and [label release page](https://deadoceans.com/releases/lost-weekend/): identify the 2026 reference; distinguish the title track from the reprise and album as a whole.
- [Published track-6 credits](https://pitchfork.com/story/read-the-full-credits-to-phoebe-bridgers-lost-weekend/): supports the named production team, instrument/programming branches, and credited Paul Lansky sample shown in the prototype. Credits establish contributions, not who proposed each choice or the order of work. Track-specific production roles should still be checked against original liner notes before a finished lesson asset.
- [Princeton’s Paul Lansky profile](https://dof.princeton.edu/people/paul-lansky): supports the 1988 work and its processed-speech context. This gives a direct historical branch through a credited sample, not a generic electronic-music genealogy.

Search surfaced a purported first-voice-memo account and a Damien Jurado melody connection. Neither was traced to a sufficiently direct source in this pass; neither is asserted in the sketch. Reviews and fan descriptions are not our own listening observations.

## Ableton Live

- [Henke’s account of the early team](https://www.roberthenke.com/technology/ableton_live.html): preserves distinct company and development roles instead of calling every contributor a founder.
- [Behles/Henke interview](https://www.roberthenke.com/interviews/ableton.html): their musical partnership, playable studio, Max/hardware limitations, and need to perform prepared material support the Monolake → Live connection.
- [Ableton’s retrospective](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/the-evolution-of-push/): early Session View, warping, and 2001 context. Later Push history is outside this first branch.
- Current manual chapters: [Session View](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/session-view/) and [Warping](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/audio-clips-tempo-and-warping/). Present-day operations are separate from release-history claims.
