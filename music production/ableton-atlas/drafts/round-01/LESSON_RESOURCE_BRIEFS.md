This refines the [curriculum's first encounter](</Users/donovan/work/classes/music production/ableton-atlas/CURRICULUM.md#5-first-three-lesson-guides>) within the [specification](</Users/donovan/work/classes/music production/ableton-atlas/SPEC.md>) and phases 1–2 of the [build plan](</Users/donovan/work/classes/music production/ableton-atlas/BUILD_PLAN.md>). The central recommendation is to prepare a way to hear something and a way to return to it. The teacher carries continuity through a brief note. Everything else—including recording in lesson one, Atlas use, and a downloadable project—can follow the musical conversation. Assume zero student activity between lessons.

## 1. A flexible first encounter

**Musical opening:** “What would you enjoy being able to do with Live?” Use her answer and existing musicianship to choose something small enough to hear now. A phrase, a held chord, an acoustic recording, or a sound she wants to understand can each be a useful beginning. Do not ask her to choose a musician identity or commit to an ongoing piece.

**Teacher preparation, when the setup is known:** identify one available sound or recording route, the audible output, a visible stop action, and a place to keep work. Check these on the actual equipment when access permits. Have a short original teacher phrase available for demonstration if later preparation makes that possible; its absence does not prevent the first meeting. Keep teacher examples separate from the student's files. Nothing here requires purchasing a controller, installing extra instruments, or downloading a pack.

The familiar 3 / 22 / 5-minute rhythm is a teacher planning aid, not a learner contract:

| Attention | Possible use of the time | What can wait |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About 3 minutes: connect | Hear her musical interest and establish who is controlling Live. Let an audible gesture orient the conversation as soon as practical. | An interface tour; naming every visible panel |
| About 22 minutes: follow one question | Establish a working sound; let her play. If useful and time permits, capture a short idea and hear it back. Demonstrate one action, return control, and listen together. | Editing, a second instrument, both Live views, and formal vocabulary |
| About 5 minutes: leave a return point | Stop, keep the working state or phrase if saving is available, and note the recognizable Set name/location. Reopen together if practical. | Exporting, tidy library organization, and a recap presentation |

These are elastic allocations. Reduce scope as setup expands; do not accelerate the last ten minutes to satisfy the table. Notes happen at a listening or saving pause. No recording length, bar count, metronome use, number of attempts, or finished product is required. If a second sound serves a question, it can replace recording rather than become another item to cover.

### When setup takes the lesson

- **A sound works, but little time remains:** enjoy a little playing, show how to stop, and note the working audio/MIDI settings alongside any saved Set containing the sound. This is a useful return point even with no recorded phrase. Record only the configuration actually verified; do not label it fully solved if it remains intermittent.
- **The keyboard route fails but another route works:** use a teacher demonstration or a simple prepared phrase if available, then invite a musical choice. Be clear that this does not establish that her keyboard is working. Let expressive keyboard playing remain the next shared experiment.
- **No usable sound or saving remains unavailable:** finish by documenting the exact symptom, the last confirmed working step, and the next teacher-led check. An audible comparison on an available separate instrument may help, but it is not mandatory. Do not describe a file as saved or a problem as solved when it is not. Avoid beginning a long troubleshooting branch near the end merely to claim success.

The teacher retains the unresolved technical question; it does not become an installation task or practice assignment for the student. Further checks requiring her equipment can happen together next time.

### How the next lesson resumes with no preparation

The teacher reads their own note before the meeting. Begin with a short reminder: “We got this sound responding; today we can keep something you play,” or “We reached this connection problem; this is the next check.” Re-establish the output and control together.

If an idea was saved, the teacher locates it with her and they listen before explaining. If only a working sound and setup note were kept, she plays something fresh. If nothing was saved, rebuild from the observed configuration or continue the specific unresolved check; there is no missing student submission. Then follow what interests her now. Remembering terminology or navigating unaided is not an entry test, and any new question can replace the proposed activity.

## 2. Notes small enough to write during the lesson

**Student-readable teacher note:** date plus a sentence or two remembering what was heard/tried and the return point. Add a useful word, unresolved question, or one relevant link only when it helps. No empty fields need filling. “We heard… Find it again at… We can revisit…” is a writing aid, not a form shown to the student.

**Teacher-only preparation:** a separate record of the next setup check, example to prepare, or explanation to simplify. Do not put private preparation or judgments about the learner into a student recap by default.

**Optional student additions:** keep the student's words identifiable, with author/date where useful, whether written later or relayed in conversation. Do not replace the original note with the addition. No prompt, response, or reflection is required.

### One fictional example, with its audiences kept separate

**Fictional lesson A — student-readable teacher note:**

> We played a short phrase and heard it back with two sounds. The second made the pauses feel different. MIDI stores note instructions; the instrument supplies the sound. Kept both in “Window phrase,” in the lesson music folder, with clips named “First sound” and “Second sound.” Next time we can listen again and play something new.

**Fictional lesson A — separate teacher-only preparation:**

> Recheck the chosen instrument's response to touch before extending this example. Open “First sound” as the reminder. Leave note editing for a question that needs it.

**Fictional optional student addition — if one were offered:**

> Student, later: “Could the second sound arrive more gently?”

All names, actions, preferences, and locations in this example are invented. The MIDI distinction is supported by [Ableton's Live Concepts manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/live-concepts/#audio-and-midi). No lesson note was shared. A delivery medium, access permissions, and recovery method must be agreed before actual sharing; they need not be chosen to test whether two brief sentences support the next lesson.

## 3. A small prioritized resource backlog

These are six authoring candidates, not six learner activities. The first two support practical teaching; the next three are **individual demo-project briefs only** selected from existing ideas. The last adds a modest connection to music beyond the software. Each can be deferred if the lesson does not need it. IDs refer to the existing [concept inventory](</Users/donovan/work/classes/music production/ableton-atlas/CURRICULUM.md#2-the-foundational-map-42-entries>); public concept URLs do not exist yet.

### 1. “Hear Yourself”: personal setup and recovery note

**Format / timing:** a few configuration facts and, if useful, one original annotated screenshot. Essential first-lesson support at this modest scale; a designed handout is unnecessary.

**Concepts / purpose:** `hear-signal`, `hear-monitoring`, `hear-levels`; connect the actual input, instrument or recording track, and output so the working state can be recovered. The learner notices where a gesture becomes audible and what was checked when it did not.

**Original material:** only the verified configuration, device/port names, visible stop action, and an observation about response. Keep personal details private. A reusable general explanation can link from this note without publishing the personal configuration.

**Optional variation / recovery:** compare another already-available output only if useful; record and return to the known working choices. If there is no working state, keep the exact unresolved symptom instead of fictional settings.

**Checks:** full Live version/edition, OS, hardware, routing, and actual responsiveness. Device-specific steps remain pending. Do not prescribe a universal buffer size; Ableton describes a latency/CPU tradeoff and additional wireless delay in its [latency guidance](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/209072289-How-to-reduce-latency). Screenshots must exclude unrelated private information.

### 2. “Keep this and find it again”: a short return guide

**Format / timing:** a few lines taught alongside saving; one original screenshot only if location remains confusing. Essential first-lesson action if keeping work is possible; a separate resource can wait.

**Concepts / purpose:** `keep-projects`, [keep-fragments](</Users/donovan/work/classes/music production/ableton-atlas/CURRICULUM.md#keep-an-idea-without-promising-it-a-future>), later `keep-portability`. The learner notices that a recognizable idea can be kept without becoming an unfinished-song obligation.

**Original material:** a teacher-owned example with a meaningful name, visible location, and short reminder of the musical moment. Use the student's own work only in the private lesson record.

**Optional variation / recovery:** keep a separately named experiment beside the original. Return to the preserved original when the new version is confusing; do not rely on remembering a sequence of undo actions after reopening.

**Checks:** verify saving, locating, and reopening on the target setup. A Set can refer to media elsewhere; a new name alone does not ensure portability. Check the [file-management manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/managing-files-and-sets/) before writing exact steps. No student file or path belongs in the public demonstration.

### 3. “Same Notes, Different Instrument” — demo-project brief

**Timing:** later optional depth; a live version of the comparison may arise in lesson one without any download.

**Concepts / purpose:** primary `play-instruments`; related `play-audio-midi`, `edit-velocity`, `listen-compare`. Hear how a fixed performance changes with the instrument. The learner notices which parts of the gesture feel preserved and what the new sound invites her to play differently.

**Original material:** one teacher-composed MIDI phrase and two deliberately chosen, compatible instrument settings, with a short listening alternative. Preserve the same note data for the initial comparison; adjust comparison level where loudness distracts from the question.

**Optional variation / recovery:** play a new response to either sound. Keep the original phrase and both named starting states; save any personal version separately.

**Checks:** audition the actual devices, velocity behavior, levels, and transitions. Verify all device/sample dependencies and permissions; “stock” is not a compatibility or redistribution guarantee. Offer a text/listening alternative if the chosen instruments are unavailable. Do not claim the second sound must be preferred.

### 4. “Let the Sound Arrive” — demo-project brief

**Timing:** later optional depth. Produce separately only if changing one sound's response adds enough beyond resource 3; otherwise begin with its listening comparison alone.

**Concepts / purpose:** primary [shape-envelopes](</Users/donovan/work/classes/music production/ableton-atlas/CURRICULUM.md#let-a-sound-arrive-and-leave>); related `play-instruments`, `edit-notes`, `hear-monitoring`. Compare one sound with an immediate and a gradual onset. The learner notices how the arrival changes a phrase and may invite more space. Distinguish an intended sound shape from a sluggish system.

**Original material:** one sustaining sound prepared by the teacher, the shared phrase, and a fresh performance responding to the slower onset. A brief original audio comparison can stand alone.

**Optional variation / recovery:** try a longer gesture or change release in a separate comparison. Retain a named starting sound and untouched phrase; restore those when accumulated changes obscure the question.

**Checks:** device and mode remain unchosen. Verify the selected amplitude-envelope behavior, pedal state, effects, and responsiveness against its manual and in Live before making claims. Do not generalize from one preset to every instrument. Confirm included samples/patches are original or cleared.

### 5. “Make Room” — demo-project brief

**Timing:** later optional depth, chosen for a distinct musical decision beyond playing and sound response.

**Concepts / purpose:** primary [build-subtract](</Users/donovan/work/classes/music production/ableton-atlas/CURRICULUM.md#make-room-before-adding-more>); related `build-roles`, `balance-levels`, `listen-compare`. Ask what should draw attention in an original two-part passage. The learner notices different consequences of changing presence, entrance, register, or level; none is a universally correct remedy.

**Original material:** the small original phrase plus one original complementary part, with a preserved reference and a few clearly named alternatives. Compare one chosen intervention at a time. Include a short audio/text comparison for use without Live.

**Optional variation / recovery:** change a different relationship or keep the fuller original. Begin with reversible comparisons and retain the reference; do not require deleting a liked part.

**Checks:** test switching without accidental simultaneous playback, comfortable levels, required devices/media, and the method used to preserve versions. Only teacher-owned musical material goes in the public package. Keep parameter comparisons honest about what else changed.

### 6. “Hear it elsewhere”: one annotated listening reference

**Format / timing:** a short reference note with an authorized listening link; later optional depth, or teacher preparation when an interest suggests it.

**Concepts / purpose:** `listen-intention`, `listen-compare`, and whichever of `shape-envelopes` or `build-subtract` the chosen moment actually illuminates. A separate musical-context ID is pending editorial review. The learner notices an audible relationship in music beyond the demo, without needing a genre label or formal analysis.

**Original material:** our own concise listening observation and a checked time reference, plus primary-source context if making historical claims. Select the work later from the musical question; no specific artist recommendation has been made or verified here.

**Optional variation / recovery:** bring another example, describe a remembered sound, or simply listen together. If the link is unavailable, use the original teaching comparison; do not make paid access necessary.

**Checks:** verify the official/authorized listening destination, access conditions, timestamp, and any factual attribution. Link to third-party audio; do not embed or redistribute a copied recording. Keep listening observations distinguishable from sourced history.

### Reuse a phrase without creating a bulk pack

One short teacher-composed phrase can supply the fixed performance in resource 3, the sound-response comparison in resource 4, and the leading part in resource 5. Familiarity may help a listener recognize the changed relationship; this is a teaching proposal to test, not a guaranteed effect. Let a different phrase replace it whenever more useful.

Reuse the source during authoring, with clear variant names. Each eventual project remains understandable and self-contained when obtained individually from its concept; opening it never depends on owning another download. Related entries may link to the same resource. This reduces production work without creating a compulsory continuous project, sampler bundle, or download-everything offer.

## 4. Questions only when their answers matter

| When | Small question set | What the answer enables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before setup-specific preparation, or together at the first meeting | What would you like to try first? Are we using software sound, the digital piano's sound, or recording an acoustic instrument? | Select one workable starting situation; do not survey every future interest |
| When preparing/connecting that situation | What computer/OS and full Live version/edition are in use; does saving work? What keyboard/audio equipment and connections are already available? | Choose compatible operations and instruments; distinguish a playing route from a recording route |
| Before the first meeting's practical arrangement | In person or remote; who controls Live, and how will both people hear it? | Check the actual listening/control arrangement |
| Before providing a readable note | Where would a simple copy be convenient, and who should have access? | Agree delivery and distinguish student additions; no vendor decision is prescribed |

The teacher can establish technical answers by looking together. This is not an intake worksheet or student preparation requirement.

**Genuine blockers for a particular action:** no functional audio route blocks playing/hearing on that setup; disabled saving blocks keeping a new Set there; unknown version/edition blocks promising a compatible download; unknown asset rights block distributing it; undefined recipient/access blocks sharing a private note. Each blocker is narrower than the lesson or Atlas project as a whole.

**Work that can proceed now:** original conceptual writing, relationships, these briefs, the fictional note pattern, and a rehearsal of resuming from a brief record. No broad hardware purchase, complete curriculum, note platform, historical-source reread, or public site is needed for those tasks.

## 5. Evidence and remaining checks

The local planning documents and [source index](</Users/donovan/work/classes/SOURCE_INDEX.md>) were read for this draft; the paid courses were not reread and the Piano Atlas was not newly inspected. The confirmed structural reference remains [The Piano, Mapped](https://templeoflum.github.io/piano-atlas/). The lesson design and six-resource prioritization are original proposals.

Primary Ableton sources checked September 20, 2026 support these release precautions:

- Record the full version, not merely “Live 12”: Sets saved in later versions, including newer point releases, may not open in earlier ones. [Backward compatibility](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000841004-Backward-Compatibility).
- Opening is insufficient verification: unavailable edition features can disable saving/exporting. Test those actions on the intended edition. [Saving and exporting are deactivated](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/209769925-Saving-and-exporting-are-deactivated).
- Collecting project media does not collect third-party plug-ins. For these small demos, prefer a design that avoids such dependencies; still verify every included device and asset. [Transferring projects](https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/209071909-Transferring-Projects-to-another-computer).

The sources linked within resources 1–2 also support the stated latency and file-management distinctions. Device choices, specific envelope/velocity behavior, exact UI instructions, output levels, asset permissions, and every distributed package remain untested. Before release, open each actual package in a recipient-like location and supported installation, check media/playback, save a variation, reopen it, and recover the original. Text/audio alternatives need their own review. This is future authoring work, not completed verification.

**Guard against over-structuring:** do not turn the timing guide into coverage targets, the note into a mandatory reflection form, the shared phrase into a required composition, or six production candidates into six lessons. If note-taking interrupts playing, shorten the note. If two resources teach the same audible distinction, combine or defer one. If a setup session produces only a precise next check, retain that truth and resume together.
