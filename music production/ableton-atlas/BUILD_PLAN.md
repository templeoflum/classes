## 1. Settle the content blueprint

**Purpose:** decide what we are building before designing its surface.

Work:

- Test the six proposed views: Studio, Actions, Devices, Sound & Form, Approaches, and Lineages. Use finding/loading an instrument, keeping/editing a played phrase, and delay as cross-view anchors; do not force every anchor into every view.
- Declare the supported Live/OS/edition/Pack scope and inventory the complete documented shortcut and device references. Distinguish catalog completeness, written entries, and hands-on verification; none implies the others.
- Start with the learner opening Live, not the whole taxonomy. Define the nearby relationships, honest recovery branch, teacher-held return point, and zero-preparation return in the first-opening blueprint.
- Give each territory a clear scope, organizing question, and a few concrete relationships. Decide what belongs together and where a cross-link is better than another entry.
- Use the fourteen-area inventory to check coverage, not to force fourteen menus. Preserve the existing concept IDs even when editorial homes change.
- Distinguish concepts, practical operations, musical/contextual references, examples, and downloadable projects. They can connect without all becoming the same kind of page.
- Choose the first collection from the existing writing and pilot pool. Identify specific evidence or technical gaps; avoid restarting an unbounded source search.
- Keep research provenance and source paths private. Define the public content boundary before implementation.

**Deliverable:** an agreed content blueprint and a prioritized list marked “first collection,” “later,” or “needs research.” These are authoring states, never student progress labels.

**Ready to move on when:** the initial neighborhood has meaningful connections and an honest first-return behavior, provisional territory boundaries make sense, and the authoring subset is bounded. Finalizing all six territories is not a prerequisite to testing that slice. Exact visual design and technology selection are not needed for the blueprint checkpoint.

## 2. Write a representative first collection

**Purpose:** test the breadth and voice of the Atlas with real content.

Use roughly ten entries as a broader authoring pool, not a quota. The round-1 proposal supplies nine existing concepts plus a contextual research brief; it is not the first-opening script or an approved release list. For the first slice, prioritize finding, navigating, hearing, playing, capture, keeping, and device response. Pair representative musical tasks with a few repeatable operations in Live. Do not make composition instruction the first learner's default. Test broader relationships as content grows without forcing one published page per view; contextual listening remains research-pending, not a placeholder to publish.

Candidate coverage:

| View | Possible first entries |
| --- | --- |
| Studio | Follow the sound; locate clips, instruments, and saved work |
| Actions | Find/load a sound; navigate and select; capture/edit a phrase; compare/undo; save/reopen, with checked shortcut and focus context |
| Devices | A small representative instrument/effect group with mechanisms, controls, uses, and availability; the full catalog remains a separate coverage obligation |
| Sound & Form | Sound response and phrase-scale relationships; reference depth rather than required composition exercises |
| Approaches | A specific comparison of musical aims and working choices, with practitioners/works where evidence supports them |
| Lineages | One carefully sourced relationship among people, practices, or tools connected to the examples above, only when ready |

For each entry, write a minimal encounter layer—question, relationship/action, way back—and optional original explanation, vocabulary, purposeful connections, and limitations. Existing longer samples are reference-layer drafts. Include only supporting formats actually needed. Operational instructions remain distinguishable from durable musical explanations.

Sketch the audio, image, demonstration, and project briefs alongside the writing: what they should reveal, what changes, what stays fixed, and how someone returns to the starting point. Do not manufacture every resource or require a downloadable Set for every concept.

**Deliverable:** a coherent seed collection, a glossary/synonym list drawn from it, and a short prioritized resource backlog. Existing sample entries are revised, not discarded merely to start again.

**Ready to move on when:** a teacher can use the written entries in conversation, a learner can understand the main idea without knowing the term first, and related links have explicit reasons. Label unresolved compatibility and factual checks; do not present untested instructions as ready. A small first-lesson subset can proceed to phase 4 before the entire batch is finished.

## 3. Build the smallest useful Atlas

**Purpose:** make the content explorable without turning it into a learning-management system.

First choose a modest visual direction and implementation approach based on the actual entries. Adapt the Piano Atlas's content principles, not its code or styling. Different relationships may deserve different treatments; do not build six elaborate visualizations merely because there are six territories.

Build:

- A close-in first-opening neighborhood, an immediately available wider view, and clear ways back. The map is the main workspace; it is not gated by completed concepts.
- Stable entry links, ordinary-language/term search, and related concepts with reasons.
- A short encounter layer with readable optional detail; equivalent list/keyboard navigation for all essential relationships.
- A lightweight curiosity flag and a way to retrieve those interests. No levels, scores, required paths, or completion percentage.
- A clear route back from an entry to its territory or previous context.
- Distinguishable deliberate lesson/return markers and temporary navigation history. No automatic persistent activity tracking; advanced personal-route editing can wait.
- Separation between public reference content and private lesson records. A private notes tool need not live inside the Atlas.

If flags are browser-local, explain that boundary and provide a simple way to keep a readable shortlist. They must not silently masquerade as a shared record or reliable backup. Defer synchronized accounts unless actual teaching needs justify them.

Use real seed content throughout. Hide unavailable media and downloads; do not ship dead controls or imply that planned resources exist. Add audio or a small interactive explanation only when an original, checked example is available and genuinely clarifies something.

**Deliverable:** an unpublished working prototype, a simple content-update workflow, and a short record of technical and storage decisions.

**Ready to move on when:** a person can arrive beside their first opening of Live or through a direct link, find a useful action without introductory reading, follow a meaningful connection, optionally keep a place, and return without getting lost. Test the blueprint's no-sound and zero-preparation return scenarios. Check keyboard use, narrow screens, diagram alternatives, and storage behavior. Browsing works without an account or a flag; the prototype does not claim to detect or control Live.

## 4. Establish the lesson-and-notes workflow

**Purpose:** make the materials useful in a real half-hour, independently of the website.

This is a parallel teaching track, not a demand that the student validate the software through homework.

- Establish her Live version/edition, computer, keyboard/audio setup, and current musical interests before preparing setup-specific instructions.
- Prepare only the first encounter's useful subset from the existing lesson sketches. Do not reteach musicianship she already has.
- Choose a low-friction way for the teacher to keep dated notes and provide a readable student copy. Decide access, authorship, and recovery before sharing.
- Trial brief notes at natural pauses: what we noticed, where the idea is saved, a term if useful, and a question to revisit. None is a mandatory field.
- Keep private teacher preparation separate from student-readable notes. Student additions are optional and identifiable as additions.
- Rehearse—and later observe—the next lesson starting with no student reading, practice, flags, or downloads. Adjust material from in-lesson confusion and interests, not usage statistics.

**Deliverable:** a compact teacher note format, a checked sharing method, and revised lesson guidance. Track “prepared,” “rehearsed,” and “used in a lesson” honestly; a rehearsal is not evidence of student testing.

**Ready for regular use when:** note-taking does not materially interrupt playing, the teacher can resume from the record, and a student can ignore all materials without becoming behind. Actual lesson feedback continues to inform every later phase; scheduling the first lesson need not block unrelated Atlas work.

## 5. Make the first optional demos

**Purpose:** let interested learners take one concept further inside Live.

This is future production work. The current request creates the plan only; it does not start making Sets or media. When production is requested, select a small batch—initially two or three—from the existing candidate list. Possible starting choices are “Same Notes, Different Instrument,” “Let the Sound Arrive,” and “Make Room.” Choose for distinct teaching value, not just ease of producing more files.

For each chosen project:

- Use original musical material and give it one primary question.
- Provide an understandable opening state, a first thing to notice, an optional variation, and a recoverable original.
- Identify the tested Live version/edition, device dependencies, asset origins, and package version before download.
- Test the actual distributed package in a clean recipient-like location with the intended supported setup: opening, playback, referenced media, saving a variation, reopening, and recovery.
- Provide a short explanation or listening alternative for someone who cannot or does not want to open the project.
- Offer it individually from relevant entries. Several concepts can reference the same project; no bulk pack, unlock requirement, forced collection, or rate limit.

**Deliverable:** a few checked demo packages and only the supporting media/instructions they need.

**Ready to distribute when:** someone understands why to open a file before downloading it, all required assets and permissions are accounted for, compatibility is explicit, and the supplied package—not just the author's working copy—passes the checks. No demo is required for the next lesson.

## 6. Check and release a first version

**Purpose:** make a trustworthy small release, not claim completion of the whole subject.

Release checks:

- **Content:** coherent entry relationships; checked instructions and factual claims; original writing and examples; no invented universal production rules.
- **Function:** direct links, search, navigation, flags, and any available downloads work. Missing or unsupported media has a readable alternative. Existing note links survive entry revisions.
- **Accessibility and listening:** keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, text explanations for essential visual relationships, narrow-screen usability, user-started audio, and stop/volume controls where audio exists.
- **Privacy:** no student information, private preparation, local source paths, research documents, or paid course files in publishing inputs. Review a student-note share separately from an Atlas release.
- **Compatibility and ownership:** the exact released files have been tested and every included asset has a known origin and permitted use.
- **Operations:** a documented content-update process, recoverable prior release, and a way to report a confusing explanation or broken item.

**Deliverable:** an approved release candidate, known-limitations list, and brief release record. Confirm the hosting destination and public/private boundary before publishing; this plan does not itself authorize deployment or student sharing.

**Ready to release when:** these checks have recorded evidence and the user approves publication. Unfinished entries stay unpublished. A map-led Atlas with readable alternatives and no downloads is a valid first release if it already helps exploration.

## 7. Expand through use

**Purpose:** grow a connected resource, not accumulate unrelated lessons and files.

Use teacher observations, learner questions, and worthwhile musical connections to choose the next small batch. Expand the original 42-entry inventory where needed; do not fill every territory evenly for appearance's sake. Deepen histories, practices, and listening references with targeted research rather than importing course prose or genre stereotypes.

Once a cluster works, shape a focused class around it: expressive electronic instruments, recordings as playable material, arranging through contrast, performance with Live, or listening-led mixing. Add original facilitation and class-specific examples while linking back to the same canonical concepts.

Only consider more involved diagrams, interactive tools, shared annotation, or student accounts when a concrete need warrants the maintenance and privacy burden. Billing, scheduling, social features, automatic progress monitoring, and AI coaching remain outside this build plan.

**Ongoing checkpoint:** each addition has a clear musical purpose, a maintainable home, and evidence that it is ready. The amount a student browses or downloads is not a success metric.
